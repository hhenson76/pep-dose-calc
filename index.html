How this calculates, and how to save it to your phone

If this was forwarded to you: this is a calculator, not a protocol. It converts a vial strength and a volume of bacteriostatic water into a mark on a syringe. The dose tiers labelled Beginner, Standard and Aggressive are copied from one person's private reference sheet — some are drug-label or trial amounts, and some are that individual's own recorded numbers, which the badge under the peptide name tells you. None of them are recommendations, and none were reviewed by a clinician for anyone else.

Formula: units = (dose ÷ vial content) × (water in mL × 100). Combination vials calculate against the individual component, not the combined milligrams, so a 5 mg + 5 mg blend is measured against 5 mg.

Dose levels follow your sheet's Beginner, Standard and Aggressive columns. Where the sheet recovered a value from your prior conversations, that value is used ahead of the clinical-evidence tier, which is what the v3 merge was for.

Blank results mean the sheet marked that tier N/E: no defensible self-injection amount was established and no prior conversation value was recovered. The mixing math still shows, since concentration is arithmetic.

Portable use: one self-contained file, nothing loaded from the internet. Copy it to a USB stick, a phone, or a cloud folder and it works fully offline.

  • iPhone: open the link in Safari, then Share, then Add to Home Screen. It installs a real icon and opens full screen with no address bar.
  • Android: open the link in Chrome, then menu, then Add to Home screen or Install app.
  • Offline: once it has loaded a single time, it keeps working with no signal. It refreshes itself the next time you open it with a connection.
  • Windows: double-click it, or pin the file to Start. Runs from any folder or removable drive.
Reconstitution volumes for research vials are concentration math, not manufacturer instructions, and do not establish sterility, solubility, stability or equivalence to an approved formulation. This tool does arithmetic on one private reference document. It is not medical advice, and it is not a substitute for a clinician who knows the person injecting.
